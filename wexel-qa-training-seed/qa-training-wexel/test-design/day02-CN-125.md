CN-125 - Renewals -loyalty credit and deductible change at renewal
Day 2 Test Design

1.  Questions and assumptions log (question · why it matters · default you assume)

- Is "last 12 months" based on the claim date or the payment date?
  Why: A claim near the cutoff could change the discount.
  Assume: Use the date the claim was paid, looking back 12 months from term end.

- Do denied or pending claims affect the discount?
  Why: CN-710004 has a denied claim.
  Assume: Only paid claims count. Denied and unpaid claims do not.

- What if the customer had a gap between policies?
  Why: The 2- and 3-year discounts require consecutive years.
  Assume: A coverage gap resets the count.

- Is the term-end date still a valid day to accept?
  Why: We need to know exactly when the offer expires.
  Assume: Acceptance is allowed until the end of that day in the policy state's time zone.

- If a deductible is changed after the offer was sent, which amount is used?
  Why: The credit must be based on the new deductible.
  Assume: Recalculate the coverage premium and credit before acceptance.

- How do we round the credit?
  Why: A percentage may produce fractions of a cent.
  Assume: Round half-up to two decimal places. The README states this for monthly quotes, but CN-125 does not specify the credit's rounding method.

- Where does the email's old premium come from?
  Why: Current rates might not match what the customer actually paid.
  Assume: Use the amount stored on the expiring policy, not a newly calculated old premium.

- What happens if a claim is paid between sending the offer and accepting it?
  Why: The customer may no longer qualify.
  Assume: Check eligibility again before confirming the renewal.


2. What decision table will I use for loyalty-credit eligibility?

Each column (T1-T8) is a separate test. Assume a valid deductible.
"C" means the coverage premium after the deductible factor, excluding
roommate and pet fees.

Condition / Result       T1   T2   T3   T4   T5   T6   T7   T8
----------------------------------------------------------------
Consecutive years         1    2    3    4    2    3    3    2
Paid claim within 12 mo?  No   No   No   Yes  Yes  No   No   No
Denied claim only?        No   No   No   No   No   Yes  No   No
Paid claim >12 mo ago?    No   No   No   No   No   No   No   Yes
Offer still valid?        Yes  Yes  Yes  Yes  Yes  Yes  No   Yes
Eligible credit rate      0%   5%   8%   0%   0%   8%   8%   5%
Can apply credit now?     No   Yes  Yes  No   No   Yes  No   Yes

Expected:
- T1: No credit. Less than 2 consecutive years.
- T2: Credit is min(5% x C, $40).
- T3: Credit is min(8% x C, $40).
- T4: No credit, even with 4 years, because of a recent paid claim.
- T5: No credit. A paid claim also disqualifies a 2-year customer.
- T6: Credit is min(8% x C, $40). A denied claim does not count.
- T7: Customer qualifies for 8%, but the expired offer cannot be accepted.
- T8: Credit is min(5% x C, $40). A claim paid more than 12 months
  ago does not disqualify the customer.


3. What boundary values will I test, and what should happen?

A. Consecutive years

For these examples, use a $300.00 coverage premium, no extra fees,
no recent paid claim and a valid offer.

- Just before 2 full years: 0% credit; renewal = $300.00.
- Exactly 2 years: 5% credit ($15.00); renewal = $285.00.
- Just before 3 full years: still 5% ($15.00); renewal = $285.00.
- Exactly 3 years: 8% ($24.00); renewal = $276.00.
- More than 3 years: still 8% ($24.00); renewal = $276.00.

B. $40 credit cap

I used quote inputs allowed by rate table v7. Amounts below are
coverage-only premiums, without roommate or pet fees.

5% tier:
- ZIP 94107, limit $71,000, deductible $1,000:
  Coverage $798.75; credit $39.94; net $758.81 (just below cap).
- ZIP 60614, limit $80,000, deductible $500:
  Coverage $800.00; credit $40.00; net $760.00 (at cap).
- ZIP 60614, limit $89,000, deductible $1,000:
  Coverage $801.00; credit $40.00; net $761.00 (above cap).

8% tier:
- ZIP 30301, limit $69,000, deductible $2,500:
  Coverage $496.80; credit $39.74; net $457.06 (below cap).
- ZIP 94107, limit $40,000, deductible $500:
  Coverage $500.00; credit $40.00; net $460.00 (at cap).
- ZIP 30301, limit $62,000, deductible $1,000:
  Coverage $502.20; credit $40.00; net $462.20 (above cap).

C. Offer and acceptance dates

Example: a California policy has a term-end date of December 31, 2026.

- November 30: no scheduled renewal offer yet (31 days before end).
- December 1: offer should be sent (30 days before end).
- December 31, 11:59:59 PM local time: acceptance succeeds.
- January 1, 12:00:00 AM local time: acceptance is rejected;
  the policy has lapsed.

The exact end-of-day cutoff is my assumption until confirmed.


4. What are the expected amounts for the four sample policies?

Pricing used: ZIP starting with 9 = 0.0125, 6 = 0.0100,
other ZIPs = 0.0090. Deductible factors: $500 = 1.00,
$1,000 = 0.90, $2,500 = 0.80.
Roommates cost $24 each; a pet adds $36. Neither fee gets a credit.

CN-710001 - 2 years, no paid claim, one roommate
- Keep $1,000: $450.00 coverage + $24.00 fee - $22.50 credit (5%)
  = $451.50 per year.
- Change to $2,500: $400.00 coverage + $24.00 fee - $20.00 credit
  = $404.00 per year.

CN-710002 - 3 years, no paid claim, pet
- Keep $500: $800.00 coverage + $36.00 fee - $40.00 capped credit
  = $796.00 per year.
- Change to $2,500: $640.00 coverage + $36.00 fee - $40.00 capped
  credit = $636.00 per year.

CN-710003 - 4 years, paid claim 11 months ago
- Keep $500: $225.00 coverage; no credit = $225.00 per year.
- Change to $2,500: $180.00 coverage; no credit = $180.00 per year.

CN-710004 - 3 years, denied claim only, two roommates
- Keep $2,500: $1,000.00 coverage + $48.00 fees - $40.00 capped
  credit = $1,008.00 per year.
- Select $2,500 again: still $1,008.00. The deductible is already
  $2,500, so this is a no-change check.

The ticket does not tell us the actual OLD premiums stored on these
policies. If the old terms used the same v7 rates, the calculated
pre-credit amounts would be $474.00, $836.00, $225.00 and $1,048.00
respectively. Those are examples, not verified historical premiums.


5. What are my top eight tests for a 45-minute first pass?

I'd start with the calculation and eligibility checks. UI, email and
database tests would come next if those test environments are ready.

1. API - CN-710001: Check $451.50, then change deductible to $2,500
   and check $404.00. Risk: Credit calculated using the old factor.
2. API - CN-710002: Check the 8% tier, $40 cap and unchanged pet fee.
   Risk: Over-discounting the premium.
3. API - CN-710003: Check that a paid claim 11 months ago gives $0
   credit. Risk: Discount granted to an ineligible customer.
4. API - CN-710004: Check denied claim, $48 roommate fees and $40 cap.
   Risk: Wrong claim handling or discounted fees.
5. API - Run the below/at/above $40 cap examples in Question 3.
   Risk: Incorrect cap or rounding.
6. UI - Try accepting on term-end day and just after midnight.
   Risk: Customer can renew too late or is blocked too early.
7. Email - Check the offer is sent 30 days before term end and lists
   old premium, new premium, credit and deductible.
   Risk: Customer gets an incorrect or incomplete offer.
8. Database - After acceptance, check the renewed term, selected
   deductible and credit are saved correctly.
   Risk: Correct amount displayed but wrong data stored.


6. What am I not running in the first pass, and what risk am I accepting?

- Every combination of tenure, claims and deductible. Time is limited.
  Risk: A specific combination could still give the wrong premium.

- Exact 12-month claim cutoff and gaps between policies. These need
  controlled claim and policy dates.
  Risk: Customers close to the eligibility boundary could be misrated.

- Other time zones and daylight saving changes. One local-date
  example is not enough.
  Risk: Offers could expire at the wrong time in another state.

- Invalid inputs, access permissions and duplicate acceptance requests.
  I would cover these after the main calculations.
  Risk: Incorrect or unauthorized renewals, or duplicate records.

- Bulk offer sending and email formatting across devices. They need
  more test setup than the first pass allows.
  Risk: Some offers may be delayed or display incorrectly.
